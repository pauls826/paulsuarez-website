How out-of-network reimbursement works

  1. You pay for your visit at the time of the appointment.
  2. After the visit, a superbill (an itemized receipt with the codes insurers need) is available in your patient portal.
  3. You submit the superbill to your insurance company, usually through their website or app.
  4. If your plan has out-of-network mental health benefits, they reimburse you a portion directly.

Questions to ask your insurance

Before your first visit, it's worth calling the number on your insurance card and asking:

  • Do I have out-of-network benefits for outpatient mental health care?
  • What's my out-of-network deductible, and how much of it have I met?
  • After the deductible, what percentage do you reimburse?
  • Are telehealth visits covered the same way?
  • How do I submit a superbill?

Plans vary a lot, so I can't promise a specific amount back. But many patients with out-of-network benefits get a meaningful share reimbursed, especially once their deductible is met.

Payment methods

Payment is by Zelle or Venmo. I don't take credit cards. If you have a Health Savings Account, you can usually pay with your HSA card through Venmo.

Good Faith Estimate

Under federal law, if you're paying for care yourself or not using insurance, you have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected costs. Ask for one any time before your visit.

Questions about fees

How much does a visit cost?

The initial evaluation (60 minutes) is $300, and follow-up visits (30 minutes) are $300. Established patients pay their previously agreed-upon rate.

Do you take insurance?

I'm out-of-network with insurance plans. You pay $300 per visit, and after each appointment a superbill is available in your patient portal to submit to your insurance for possible out-of-network reimbursement. How much comes back depends on your plan.

What's a superbill?

A superbill is an itemized receipt with the codes your insurance company needs to process an out-of-network claim. Mine are available in your patient portal after each visit, and you submit them to your insurer yourself.

Can I use my HSA?

Usually, yes. Payment is by Zelle or Venmo, and most HSA cards can be used to pay through Venmo.
